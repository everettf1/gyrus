About this atlas

Designed and built by Everett Freeman, University of Washington, 2026.

Cortex: fsaverage6 surface (FreeSurfer) with the Destrieux 2010 parcellation. Deep structures: Harvard-Oxford subcortical atlas (FSL), AAL3 (Rolls et al. 2020), the Pauli 2018 subcortical atlas and the Juelich histological atlas, which also gives the white-matter tracts. Fine cortical areas: HCP-MMP1.0 (Glasser et al. 2016). Networks: Yeo et al. 2011, drawn from the Schaefer 2018 parcellation. Every study quoted in an evidence card was checked against its PubMed record. The MRI in the cut view is the MNI152 2009 template (Fonov et al. 2011). Function maps come from NeuroQuery (Dockès et al. 2020), blood supply from the Johns Hopkins arterial territories atlas (Liu et al. 2023, CC BY-SA 4.0), and the variability maps from the Juelich probabilistic maps of 10 post-mortem brains. Text written for this atlas from the references listed with each region.

    How to use it

    Drag to rotate and scroll to zoom. Click any region to read about it, or search for it by name. The row of decks at the top decides which set of regions the brain shows: lobes, functional areas, gyri, sulci, deep structures, thalamic nuclei or the brain stem and cerebellum.

    Learn walks through a deck one region at a time. Type shows a highlighted region and asks for its name. Find names a region and asks you to click it. Quiz gives a clue about function or a clinical sign and asks for the region.

    In Type, Practice shows the name, Recall hides it (press ? for one letter at a time), Rush is 60 seconds with combos, Test goes through 10, 25 or all regions once, and Daily is the same 10 regions for the day, one try per deck. Practice and Recall can also run for 60 seconds or 10 questions. Right answers go in on their own; Tab then Enter restarts. If you type the name of a different region, that region lights up in red so you can see where it really is. Progress shows what you know, your bests and your weakest regions.

    The sliders on the right fade the cortex to show deep structures, inflate the cortex to open the sulci, and cut the brain along the chosen plane. A cut shows the real MRI at that plane, labelled, with a matching 2D slice you can hover, click and scroll. Search a function such as memory, fear or faces to color the regions most linked with it in imaging studies. Pathways steps a signal through the brain station by station, and Blood supply colors each artery’s territory. Regions from the Juelich atlas have a variability map showing how much their borders differ between people. Keys 1 to 6 change the view, I inflates, H switches hemisphere, and / jumps to search.
